Suncast 3D guide

Sun position, building shadows and hours of direct sunlight for any address.

Suncast 3D places the sun where it really is for a given location, date and time, and renders the shadows that buildings and terrain cast at that moment. This page covers the controls, how to read the results, and what the model can and cannot tell you.

1. Choose a location

  • Address — type an address or place name in the search box at the top of the panel and pick a suggestion.
  • Coordinates — enter latitude and longitude, then Go (or press Enter in either field).
  • Locate — uses the browser's geolocation. Requires a secure page (HTTPS) and permission.
  • Click the map — selects the spot under the cursor without moving the camera.

The selected building is tinted cyan and its street address appears under the coordinates. The UTC offset is set automatically from the location's real time zone, including daylight saving for the chosen date; it can be overridden with the slider.

2. Set the date and time

  • Date — the date field, or the shortcuts for today, the March and September equinoxes and the June and December solstices.
  • Time — the slider fixed along the bottom of the screen, in 15-minute steps. It stays available when the panel is closed.
  • Play — ▶ runs the day at one hour per second. Moving the slider pauses it.

The slider track is coloured to show night, twilight and daylight for that date and location.

3. Read the sun readout

☀️ Sun 37.5° above the horizon · bearing 146° SE · a 10 m house casts a 13 m shadow ↑ 07:34 ↓ 19:59
ValueMeaning
Altitude — 37.5° above the horizonThe sun's height. 0° is the horizon, 90° is overhead. Low sun produces long shadows.
Bearing — 146° SEThe direction the light comes from, 0° being north and 90° east. A facade receives sun when the bearing points at it.
Shadow of a 10 m house — 13 mA scale reference for the sun's height. At 45° a building's shadow equals its height; at 20°, about three times its height. The value scales with height.
↑ 07:34 ↓ 19:59Sunrise and sunset in local time for the selected date.

Below the horizon the readout switches to Night and gives the sun's angle below it.

4. Hours of direct sun

☀ Direct sun at this spot on 17 Sep: 10 h 00 of 12 h 15 daylight · in shadow: 07:45–10:00

Daylight hours are the same across a town; what differs is how much of that light reaches a specific spot rather than being blocked by nearby buildings. Every 15 minutes between sunrise and sunset, a ray is cast from 1.2 m above the selected point towards the sun and tested against the 3D buildings and the terrain. Blocked samples are merged into the shaded intervals shown at the end of the line.

The figure is recomputed when the location, the date or the time zone changes. Comparing 21 June (highest sun, shortest shadows) with 21 December (lowest sun) gives the two extremes of the year.

5. Sun path

With Show sun path enabled, a gold arc over the selected spot traces the sun's track for that day, from sunrise to sunset, with a marker at the time currently set. Two short lines on the ground point to the sunrise and sunset directions and carry their times. The arc is a diagram drawn at a fixed radius; it casts no shadow.

The embed shows the 3D view and the time slider without the control panel.

8. Map data

The default source is OpenStreetMap 3D buildings with satellite imagery and terrain, which requires nothing from the visitor. Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles can be loaded instead from the Map data section by entering your own Google Cloud key; that key stays in your browser. Google does not serve Photorealistic 3D Tiles to accounts based in the EU/EEA.

9. Accuracy and limits

ElementStatus
Sun position, sunrise, sunset, day lengthAstronomically accurate, using the location's real time zone including daylight saving.
TerrainReal elevation data; hills and slopes shade correctly.
Building footprintsFrom OpenStreetMap. Coverage is extensive in much of Europe.
Building heightsApproximate — most buildings carry no height tag, so a default height is extruded. Shadow direction and timing are reliable; the length of a given building's shadow is an estimate.
Trees, hedges, walls, balconies, roof overhangsNot modelled.
Windows and interiorsNot modelled. Results describe what reaches the facade or the plot.

Suitable for assessing orientation, timing and obstruction. Not a substitute for a surveyed shadow study.

10. Typical uses

  • Property — check the hours of direct sun on a plot, balcony or facade before a viewing, and compare summer with winter.
  • Garden — sample several points; vegetables and fruit generally need six or more hours of summer sun, shade-tolerant planting three to four.
  • Solar panels — identify roof areas with unobstructed midday sun in winter, as a first filter before a yield study.
  • Photography and events — find when light reaches a facade or an outdoor area on a given date.

Questions

How many hours of direct sunlight does a spot get?

Select the spot and read the direct-sun line: it gives the hours of sun and the shaded intervals for the chosen date, with surrounding buildings taken into account.

How can I tell whether a neighbouring building blocks the sun in winter?

Set the date to 21 December and compare with 21 June. A large drop, or shaded intervals covering midday, indicates an obstruction; the sun path arc shows which side it is on.

How accurate is the shadow model?

The astronomy and the terrain are accurate. Building footprints are real, but heights are mostly default values and vegetation is absent, so shadow lengths are estimates.

What does the bearing mean?

The compass direction the light comes from: 0° north, 90° east, shown in degrees and as a compass point.

Can it be embedded in another website?

Yes — see section 7 for the iframe and its parameters.

Is anything recorded about my searches?

Keys and locations stay in the browser. Usage statistics are cookieless and never include coordinates.
